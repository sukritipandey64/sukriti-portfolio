In progress · Research phase

Digital Barista (working title)

Ordering coffee shouldn't feel like an oral exam.

Role
Solo — research, product design, build
Platform
Phone web app, opened from a café table QR code, no download
Timeline
Oct 2026 – ongoing

01 — The insight

I still ask the barista what would you recommend? Tasting notes confuse me too.

  • Specialty coffee is growing fast in India, but menus assume you already speak coffee: brew methods, estates, processing, roast levels, tasting notes.
  • Beginners feel judged asking the barista, so they default to a hazelnut latte, or guess.
  • Tasting notes describe the coffee, not whether you'll like it. You can love blueberries and hate blueberry coffee.

The barrier isn't information. It's asking.

02 — The ideaScan the QR code on the table, say what you usually drink, and get two picks from today's menu, explained in plain words. A two-tap check-in after the cup makes the next pick better.

03 — Decisions so far

No prompt needed

Scan, one tap, two picks. Good help shouldn't require knowing what to ask.

One onboarding question

"What do you usually order?" It starts from what people already know.

Always two picks: safe and stretch

The user keeps the choice, and a miss is framed as part of the experiment.

Tasting notes, translated

Into Indian food memories: jamun, jaggery, filter coffee. Unfamiliar language becomes familiar taste.

A two-tap check-in

It returns the next suggestion right away. The tone: come back and we'll explore together.

Cold start

Seeded with barista knowledge and a small tasting session. Real reactions take over as data grows.

Why not just ask a chatbot?

Picks come from the café's actual menu today. And the record of how beginners react to specific coffees is data no general chatbot has.

04 — Research, before building

My last project taught me to validate before I build. This time, research comes first.

Conversations with baristas, café founders and coffee educators have started. Early signals are below.

The outreach asks about the problem, not the solution. Alongside it, I'm reading reel comments by hand to hear how beginners describe the experience in their own words.

Questions I'm trying to answer

  • What makes beginners hesitate at the counter?
  • Where do they look for help today, if anywhere?
  • What do baristas wish customers felt comfortable asking?

Hypotheses to test — not findings

  • The barrier is social, not informational.Early signal: people hand the decision to the barista
  • Beginners want a starting point, not an education.Early signal: they start from what they already drink
  • Baristas would welcome better questions from customers.

05 — Early signals

A specialty café co-founder in Chennai, on what happens at the counter.

The conversations have started, and this is what's coming through so far. Each point is a signal to check against the next conversations, not a finding. Two already echo what I'd seen elsewhere: people asking "what do you recommend?", and confusion over tasting notes.

What it showsIn his wordsWhat it changes
The decision gets handed overA lot of them just end up asking ‘what do you recommend?’That moment is the problem. The app gives the recommendation people would otherwise have to ask a stranger for.
People start from the familiarMost people look for something familiar first, like a cappuccino or a cold coffee.The first question stays "what do you usually drink?", and the safe pick stays close to the answer.
Three questions, every dayWhat's the difference from espresso? Which is less bitter? Are the tasting notes added flavours?Every pick answers all three. Bitterness becomes the main scale to compare on, and each card says the notes come from the bean, nothing added.
The words that trip peopleSome don't know what a specific processing is, like a washed, natural or honey… or a thermal shock.The plain-words glossary covers processing methods, not just flavours.
No two menus alikeWe don't add any flavour into our coffee… we do cream tops or cold foams.A hazelnut cold brew fan has no exact match here. Picks have to come from each café's own menu, not a generic drink list.

The tension I'm working through

I don't force anyone to get into specialty coffee… Once they ask a question, we go on explaining.

Good baristas invite curiosity; they don't push it. So the stretch pick has to read as an invitation, never a nudge, and the safe pick is always there.

One step at a time, the way he does it

  1. A milk drinkcappuccino, latte, cold coffee
  2. A cortadoless milk, more coffee
  3. A pour-overblack, when they're open to it

This becomes the logic for the stretch pick.
