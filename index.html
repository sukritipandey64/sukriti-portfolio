03 — In practice

Four and a half years
of work that shipped.

Most of what I've built lives inside a firm, on internal systems, used by people on a Tuesday morning without a second thought. I can't show the screens. I can tell you what the problems were and what I did about them.

Deloitte, US Offices — 2022 to now. Intern to UX Designer 2. Often the solo designer-developer on the project.

Project tracker

60+ people · 4 teams · 150+ projects

A team ran its whole pipeline through spreadsheets and status calls. I pitched, designed and built the tool that replaced it — ownership across sub-teams, completion flows, threaded notes. Live for six months and counting, in daily use. That's the only metric I really trust.

Training people finish because they want to, not because a compliance deadline is coming. I map which core drives a module actually needs, then build the mechanics that serve them. Behaviour design with a deadline attached.

I don't write code from scratch anymore. I still ship — I direct AI tooling through the build, debug it, and put it in production, and I teach my team to do the same. The skill isn't the syntax. It's knowing what should exist, and recognising when the output is wrong.
