03 — In practice

Four and a half years
of work that shipped.

Most of what I've built lives inside a firm, on internal systems, used by people on a Tuesday morning without a second thought. I can't show the screens. I can show you what changed.

Deloitte, US Offices — 2022 to now. Intern to UX Designer 2. Often the sole designer-developer, from requirements to production.

Project tracker

60+people using it daily

A team ran its pipeline through spreadsheets and status calls. I led discovery with the team leads, then designed and built the tracker that replaced it. I still own it after launch, running the feedback and release cycles that grew it from an intake form into the platform the team runs on.

Product ownership · Systems · Shipped

Design-to-production pipeline

40h → 10hper animated microsite

A workflow that connects Figma straight to production code through MCP servers: components and auto-layout set up in Figma Make, refined in Figma Design, then generated as deployable HTML. Build time alone fell from about 30 hours to 2. I direct the AI tooling through the build, debug it, check it by hand and put it in production, and I teach my team to work the same way.

AI-directed build · Front-end · Enablement

Review platform

178+requests managed

A review process that lived in email threads became one platform: multi-document uploads, and separate interfaces for the people asking and the people approving. Every request now has a status and an owner.

Complex workflows · Role-based UX · Build

Intranet hub

130,000+unique homepage visitors

A key contributor to an eight-site hub built during a business restructuring. The job was information architecture: taking content scattered across old sites and giving it a structure people could navigate.

Information architecture · Design at scale

Research-led collaboration

9cross-team collaborations

Ten to fifteen interviews about why teams didn't work across boundaries, then a low-friction way for them to start. I iterated against adoption signals, not opinions.

User research · Behaviour · Iteration

Gamified learning

~40hdevelopment time saved

Training people finish because they want to, not because a deadline is coming. I map which Octalysis core drives a module needs, then build the mechanics that serve them, including a Jeopardy-style game taken from concept to code.

Behavioural design · Gamification · Interaction

Automation migration

8–10tools rebuilt in two months

Led the move from Nintex to Kwiz against a two-month deadline: rebuilt the team's automation tools on the new platform and upskilled two teammates to run them.

Tooling · Delivery · Enablement

Making UX an offering

0 → 1UX as a formal team capability

UX wasn't a service on my team. With one senior colleague I made the case to leadership and defined what we'd offer and what good looked like, then delivered enough of it that people started asking for it by name. I also lead the team's AI adoption, which senior leadership has recognised.

Practice building · Stakeholders · Advocacy
